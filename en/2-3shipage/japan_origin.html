Chinese Dishes Born in Japan

A dedicated guide to unique 'Chuka' classics that were invented or radically reimagined within Japan.

Tenshindon (Tenshinhan): A fluffy crabmeat omelet draped over steamed white rice, topped with a glistening sweet-and-savory soy glaze. Created during the Taisho period in Tokyo or Osaka.

Hiyashi Chuka: Chilled ramen noodles dressed with colorful ribbons of egg crepe, cucumber, and ham, bathed in a refreshing sweet soy-vinegar dressing. Conceived in Sendai in 1937 as an antidote to summer heat.

Chukadon: A savory stir-fry of pork, seafood, and assorted vegetables simmered in thickened broth served over rice. Originated as staff canteen meals in Showa-era Tokyo.

Japanese Chili Prawns (Ebi Chili): Adapted from authentic Sichuan spicy dry-braised prawns by Master Chef Chen Kenmin, adding tomato paste and egg to create a mildly sweet and spicy harmony.
